# Experiment: Two Words with Overlapping Support

## Motivation

We want to observe what the normal form algorithm does when two words have
*partially overlapping* support — they share some generators but not all —
and where some of those generators sit inside a hyperedge while others are
isolated vertices.

---

## Hypergraph χ

```
Vertices (in order):  a, b, c, d, e
Hyperedge:            H = {b, c, d}
Dense closure of H:   {∅, {b}, {c}, {d}, {b,c}, {b,d}, {c,d}, {b,c,d}}
Isolated vertices:    a, e  (not in any non-trivial edge)
```

Augmented alphabet symbols generated by E2 = {{b,c}, {b,d}, {c,d}}:

| E2 edge  | Symbol    |
|----------|-----------|
| {b, c}   | e_{bc}    |
| {b, d}   | e_{bd}    |
| {c, d}   | e_{cd}    |

### Support classification

| Generator | In edge H? | In w₁? | In w₂? | Role               |
|-----------|-----------|--------|--------|--------------------|
| a         | no        | yes    | no     | w₁-exclusive, free |
| b         | yes       | yes    | yes    | shared, in edge    |
| c         | yes       | no     | yes    | w₂-exclusive, in edge |
| d         | yes       | yes    | no     | w₁-exclusive, in edge |
| e         | no        | no     | yes    | w₂-exclusive, free |

- Shared support: {b} — sits inside the hyperedge
- Each word has one in-edge exclusive generator and one free exclusive generator

---

## Words

Both words have length 6, each generator appearing exactly twice.

```
w₁ = a · b · d · a · d · b       support: {a, b, d}
w₂ = b · c · e · c · b · e       support: {b, c, e}
```

As hyperwords (via σ, each letter a singleton hyper-letter):

```
w₁ = [a] [b] [d] [a] [d] [b]
w₂ = [b] [c] [e] [c] [b] [e]
```

---

## Normal Forms

### w₁ = a·b·d·a·d·b

```
NF(w₁) = [a] · [b d] · [a] · [b d e_{bd}⁻¹]
```

Block-by-block:

| Block | Contents            | Notes                                   |
|-------|---------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| 1     | [a]                 | isolated vertex, stays singleton        |
| 2     | [b d]               | b and d merged — both in H              |
| 3     | [a]                 | second a, isolated                      |
| 4     | [b d e_{bd}⁻¹]     | b and d merged again with commutator correction |

The e_{bd}⁻¹ in block 4 is the commutator correction from reordering the
second occurrences of b and d past the first pair.

### w₂ = b·c·e·c·b·e

```
NF(w₂) = [b c] · [e] · [b c e_{bc}⁻¹] · [e]
```

Block-by-block:

| Block | Contents            | Notes                                   |
|-------|---------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| 1     | [b c]               | b and c merged — both in H              |
| 2     | [e]                 | isolated vertex, stays singleton        |
| 3     | [b c e_{bc}⁻¹]     | b and c merged again with commutator correction |
| 4     | [e]                 | second e, isolated                      |

The e_{bc}⁻¹ in block 3 is the commutator correction from reordering the
second occurrences of b and c past the first pair.

## Commutator [w₁, w₂] = w₁⁻¹ · w₂⁻¹ · w₁ · w₂

```
Input (24 letters):
  B D A D B A  E B C E C B  a b d a d b  b c e c b e
```

Normal form (16 blocks, 56 rewriting steps):

```
NF([w₁,w₂]) =
  b⁻¹ d⁻¹  |  a⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bd}⁻¹  |  a⁻¹
  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹ e_{bc}⁻¹
  |  a  |  b d  |  a  |  b c d e_{bc} e_{bd}⁻¹ e_{cd}⁻¹
  |  b  |  e  |  b c e_{bc}⁻¹  |  e
```

The commutator is **not the identity** — w₁ and w₂ do not commute.

Block 12 (`b c d e_{bc} e_{bd}⁻¹ e_{cd}⁻¹`) is the most striking: it contains
all three vertices of the hyperedge H = {b,c,d} together with all three edge
symbols, both in positive and negative form. This is where the interaction
between the two words gets recorded.

The first 8 blocks shadow NF(w₁⁻¹) and NF(w₂⁻¹); the last 8 shadow NF(w₁)
and NF(w₂). The commutator structure does not collapse, so the group element
[w₁, w₂] is genuinely non-trivial.

## Is the commutator central? Nested commutators

To test whether c = [w₁, w₂] is central in the subgroup ⟨w₁, w₂⟩ we compute
the two nested commutators [w₁, c] and [w₂, c]. If both are the identity then
c commutes with every generator of the subgroup, hence is central.

Each input is 60 letters (6 + 24 + 6 + 24).

### [w₁, c] = w₁⁻¹ · c⁻¹ · w₁ · c

151 rewriting steps, **32 blocks** — not the identity.

```
NF([w₁, c]) =
  b⁻¹ d⁻¹  |  a⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bd}⁻¹  |  a⁻¹
  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bc}⁻¹ e_{bd}
  |  b⁻¹  |  a⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bd}⁻¹  |  a⁻¹
  |  b c  |  e  |  b c e_{bc}⁻¹  |  e
  |  a  |  b d  |  a  |  b d e_{bd}⁻¹
  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹ e_{bc}⁻¹
  |  a  |  b d  |  a  |  b c d e_{bc} e_{bd}⁻¹ e_{cd}⁻¹
  |  b  |  e  |  b c e_{bc}⁻¹  |  e
```

### [w₂, c] = w₂⁻¹ · c⁻¹ · w₂ · c

143 rewriting steps, **40 blocks** — not the identity.

```
NF([w₂, c]) =
  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹ e_{bc}⁻¹
  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bc}⁻¹ e_{bd}
  |  b⁻¹  |  a⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bd}⁻¹  |  a⁻¹
  |  b c  |  e  |  b c e_{bc}⁻¹  |  e
  |  a  |  b d  |  a  |  b c d e_{bc} e_{bd}⁻¹ e_{cd}⁻¹
  |  b  |  e  |  b c e_{bc}⁻¹  |  e
  |  b⁻¹ d⁻¹  |  a⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bd}⁻¹  |  a⁻¹
  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹  |  e⁻¹  |  b⁻¹ c⁻¹ e_{bc}⁻¹
  |  a  |  b d  |  a  |  b c d e_{bc} e_{bd}⁻¹ e_{cd}⁻¹
  |  b  |  e  |  b c e_{bc}⁻¹  |  e
```

### Conclusion

**The commutator c = [w₁, w₂] is not central** in ⟨w₁, w₂⟩. Both nested
commutators are non-trivial, so the subgroup generated by w₁ and w₂ is not
2-step nilpotent — its lower central series does not stabilize after one
commutator.

A tell-tale block appearing in both nested normal forms is
`b⁻¹ c⁻¹ d⁻¹ e_{bc}⁻¹ e_{bd}` (block 8 in each). This hyper-letter involves
all three vertices of H = {b,c,d} together with mixed-sign edge symbols — a
genuinely new configuration not seen in NF(w₁), NF(w₂), or NF(c) alone,
showing that the deeper interaction keeps generating new group-theoretic content.

---

## Observations

1. **In-edge generators merge into hyper-letters.** The pairs (b,d) in w₁ and
   (b,c) in w₂ each end up grouped into the same block, because the rewriter
   can move them together (they share the hyperedge H).

2. **Free generators stay singleton.** Both `a` and `e` are isolated, so they
   cannot be merged with anything; they appear as [a] and [e] throughout.

3. **Commutator symbols appear only for in-edge pairs.** e_{bd} shows up in
   w₁'s last block; e_{bc} shows up in w₂'s third block. No commutator
   appears for pairs involving the isolated generators a or e.

4. **The shared generator b participates in different merges.** In w₁ it merges
   with d (forming [b d]); in w₂ it merges with c (forming [b c]). The
   differing exclusive in-edge partner drives which commutator symbol appears.
